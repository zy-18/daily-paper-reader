# Prescribed Performance Guided Safe Reinforcement Learning for Roundabout Navigation of Autonomous Vehicles 

Wenhan Li †, Yangrui Zhang †, Hang Zhou, Peiran Liu, and Yiding Ji ∗

Abstract — Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs) face significant challenges in roundabout navigation due to dense traffic interactions and unpredictable agent behaviors, ne-cessitating high levels of adaptability and safety. While the combination of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Model Pre-dictive Control (MPC) offers a promising approach, exist-ing frameworks suffer from a structural mismatch where pointwise RL decisions fail to align with MPC’s trajectory level optimization, and often lack rigorous safety guarantees. This work proposes RLTrajGen-PPMPC-CBF, a hierarchical framework for safe, smooth and comfort roundabout naviga-tion, which regulates the coupling between RL-based planning and MPC-based control. We employ Prescribed Performance Constraints (PPC) to enforce a gradual, stable convergence between reference trajectories and control outputs, ensuring MPC tracking performance remains consistent with intended behavior from RL. Additionally, a Control Barrier Function (CBF) layer provides real-time safety corrections that are fed back into the RL process. Simulation results in the MetaDrive environment demonstrate that our method achieves a 6.25% increase in average success rate and a 65.87% reduction in Motion Sickness Dose Value (MSDV) compared to the optimal baseline, meanwhile accelerating training convergence. 

Index Terms — Connected and autonomous vehicles, deep reinforcement learning, model predictive control, prescribed performance control, control barrier functions 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The advancement of Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) has accelerated the deployment of Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs), enabling them to navigate diverse urban environments [1]–[4]. Significant progress has been achieved in standard driving tasks, including lane keeping [5]–[7], lane changing [8], [9], and adaptive cruise control [10], [11]. However, roundabouts remain particularly challenging due to dense vehicle interactions and rapidly changing traffic conditions, where planning or control fail-ures may compromise both safety and ride comfort [5], [12]. Current autonomous driving approaches mainly fall into learning-based and model-based methods. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated strong capability in high-dimensional perception and decision-making [13]–[16]. However, its model-free nature makes it difficult to explicitly incorporate vehicle dynamics and safety constraints [17],

In contrast, Model Predictive Control (MPC) explicitly considers system dynamics and constraints, while Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) have been widely integrated into MPC to provide formal safety guarantees [19]–[22]. Nev-ertheless, model-based approaches generally exhibit limited adaptability in highly dynamic environments. To combine the adaptability of RL with the capability to enforce safety and other constraints of MPC, hybrid RL-MPC frameworks have recently attracted increasing atten-tion [23]–[26]. Oliveira et al. [25] employed RL to determine discrete decision variables to accelerate MPC optimization, while Lubars et al. [26] combined DDPG with MPC, where MPC served as a safety backup for potentially unsafe RL actions. Zhang et al. [8] further incorporated a CBF safety filter into an RL-MPC framework (Fig. 1(b)). Nevertheless, these methods still suffer from a structural mismatch: RL produces pointwise decisions whereas MPC optimizes tra-jectories, limiting MPC’s ability to accurately realize the planning intent and resulting in less smooth trajectories in complex driving scenarios. Zhang et al. [8] introduced a CBFG-PPO framework (Fig. 1 (b)), where RL provides target states for MPC, and CBF filters unsafe actions and feeds corrections back to RL. Nevertheless, RL produces pointwise decisions while MPC optimizes over trajectories in this framework, which results in structural mismatch and limits MPC’s ability to accurately realize RL intent, producing less smooth trajectories for CAVs in complex environments. To address the mentioned issues, we propose a novel RLTrajGen-PPMPC-CBF framework, as illustrated in Fig. 1 (c), and validate its effectiveness in a roundabout scenario. The proposed framework consists of three key components: an RL-based Trajectory Generator (RLTrajGen) that facil-itates high-level decision making by producing reference arXiv:2610.27732v1 [cs.RO] 20 Oct 2026 trajectories, a prescribed performance MPC (PPMPC) that dynamically adjusts trajectory tracking within a predefined performance envelope, and a CBF-based safety filter that corrects unsafe behaviors. Specifically, we first introduce a trajectory generator that transforms the pointwise outputs of RL into reference trajectories for MPC tracking. In addition, we incorporate a prescribed performance constraint (PPC) mechanism to progressively reduce the tracking error, enabling MPC to more accurately follow the trajectories generated by RL. This design improves the consistency between planning and control, facilitating more stable policy learning and enhanced navigation performance. The main contributions of this work are summarized below: 1) We design a trajectory generation mechanism that con-verts single-step outputs from RL into trajectories with adaptive parameters for MPC tracking, thereby effectively resolving the structural mismatch between RL and MPC and significantly accelerating the convergence of training. 2) We utilize PPC to regulate the coupling between RL-based planning and MPC-based control, which is the first one in RL-MPC hierarchical framework to our best knowledge. Our method facilitates the progressive reduc-tion of trajectory tracking errors between the planning and control layers, thus balancing the adaptability to complex scenarios and the safety for autonomous systems, and im-proving ride comfort by producing smoother trajectories. D. CBF-based safety filter 

CBF is employed to synthesize a safety filter to correct potentially unsafe behaviors generated by MPC. In a round-about scenario, the primary safety objective is to avoid inter-vehicle collisions. To this end, each vehicle is approximated by an ellipse, which enables a quantitative formulation of collision-avoidance constraints. As illustrated in Fig. 4, a vehicle is first approximated by a rectangle and then enclosed by a circumscribed ellipse. Let a and b denote the semi-major and semi-minor axes, respectively. xo) = 1. Then, let Qθ = RTθ DR θ . To compute the distance between two ellipses, consider the line connecting their centers, along which a point is given by x = xo + et,where e is the unit direction vector. The roundabout simulation environment is built in MetaDrive and is illustrated in Fig. 5, with some key parameters specified as follows. The computer running rein-forcement learning training is equipped with an AMD EPYC 7453 processor, with CUDA version 12.9 and Python version 3.11.4. During training, four parallel environments are used for sampling, followed by training on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. A fixed random seed is set at the initialization of each process to ensure the reproducibility of the experiments. The key hyperparameters of the framework listed in Fig. 5 have been manually carefully tuned.

2) Effect of the PPMPC module: M5 is obtained by replacing the MPC module in M3 with PPMPC. This leads to a substantial change in vehicle behavior: the overall profiles of velocity, heading angle, acceleration, and steering angle reduce to fewer oscillations, resulting in a smoother trajectory in Fig. 9. This improvement arises because tra-ditional MPC often neglects the cost induced by trajectory tracking errors, focusing instead on reducing control effort, which can gradually deteriorate performance. Meanwhile, constraining trajectory tracking errors with fixed weights is generally impractical: if the weight is set too small, MPC may ignore trajectory errors, whereas if it is set too large, the optimization problem may become infeasible. Therefore, the PPMPC module needs to be integrated with RL for more flexible parameters. By comparing the average success rate of M5 and M3 , PPMPC substantially improves safety, increasing the success rate from 77.50% to 96.25%. In addition, PPMPC achieves markedly better performance in terms of MSDV and JERK than M3 , further demonstrating enhanced ride comfort. V. C ONCLUSION 

This work proposes a hierarchical learning based control framework for roundabout navigation of CAVs. Our approach tackles the oscillation issues caused by the structural mis-match between RL and MPC to facilitate smoother and more gradual decision-making. Trajectories are generated from RL policies and utilized to solve an MPC problem with PPC defined costs. Safety is ensured by a CBF layer, allowing the agent to learn the revised actions. Simulations on the MetaDrive platform validate the superior performance of our algorithm through the construction and modular comparison of five baseline methods. Our framework not only achieves the highest average reward and success rate, but also returns the smoothest trajectory measured by MSDV and JERK, demonstrating its potential for real-world driving scenarios.

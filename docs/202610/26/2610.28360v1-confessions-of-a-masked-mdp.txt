# Abstract 

In reinforcement learning, one presumes the action set to be known ahead of time, and, thus, one must know if any state-action pairs are not permissible ahead of computations. In some applications, determining this ac-tion mask is the main computational bot-tleneck. Isolating this problem to highlight its core properties, we study how to com-bine the search for an optimal policy with the learning of the mask itself, under a lin-ear MDP model in which each attempted ac-tion reveals the exact value of a linear score determining its validity. In this framework, we first construct an algorithm based on de-terministic optimistic optimization and show that it is minimax optimal, which we show is also computationally efficient. However, it is sub-optimal on non-degenerate instances, as we show. We propose an alternative, which is also computationally efficient, which plans its exploration strategy. This algorithm ad-mits a formal interpretation as an analogue of information-directed-sampling for determin-istic feedback, which may be of independent interest. Finally, we study an application of this framework to transfer learning, where the invalid action set changes over time. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Reinforcement learning, the interactive learning of control systems and games (Tesauro, 1995; Sutton and Barto, 2018), and in particular its formalisation us-ing Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) (Puterman, 1994), has been an undeniably successful field of re-search in the last thirty years. Applications span far and wide, from datacenter cooling (Gao et al., 2014), to autonomous driving (Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2016), through advertising (Cai et al., 2017) and conversa-tional AI training (Li et al., 2016). In the MDP formalisation, the set of available actions is typically assumed to be independent of the state of the system. In the classical theory (Bellman, 1957), this assumption is a convenience and varying actions can be accommodated at the cost of some burdensome notation (see, e.g. , Puterman, 1994, Ch. 6). In computation, however, ever since RL methods have shifted to GPU-accelerated computation (Mnih et al., 2015), varying actions have become nefarious spoilers. Indeed, GPU-acceleration of, e.g. , Value Iteration, re-lies on the ability to broadcast operations over the en-tire state-action space. The solution, early adopted by practitioners, is to use a boolean invalid action mask to zero-out the rewards (and/or transitions) cor-responding to invalid actions. Hadamard multiplica-tion by the mask preserves the tensor structure, and is accelerated. Remarkably, there has been some theoret-ical work showing that masking preserves the conver-gence of Policy Gradient (Huang and Ontanon, 2020). This state of affairs is acceptable in many applications. However, in some cases, computing the mask is itself the computational bottleneck. For instance, in civil engineering applications, each action corresponds to a refinement of the current design (state), and determin-ing whether it satisfies the project’s design constraints may require extensive simulations. Precomputing all possible designs in order to check their feasibility for a building is, quite evidently, a prohibitively expensive approach to architecture. When pre-computation of the mask is impossible, the current approach is to penalise invalid actions through reward shaping Ng et al. (1999), which is a form of Lagrangian relaxation. However, when it comes to learning, this approach is fundamentally flawed: it treats the deterministic signal of permissibility and the stochastic reward signal homogeneously, a dilu-tion which explains the terrible performance of these as learning methods. One would expect the literature on constrained MDPs to have addressed this problem somehow. Yet, both arXiv:2610.28360v1 [stat.ML] 20 Oct 2026 classical (Altman, 1999) and more recent (Achiam et al., 2017; Amani et al., 2021) work focuses on ex-pected costs, with the aim of controlling violations. In our setting, due to determinism of signals, there is no reason to repeat a known violation. The closest work to ours is, thus, that of Wei et al. (2023), whose in-stantaneous hard constraints are in the same spirit, but whose regret bounds are dominated by the min-imax analysis and stochasticity of the rewards in the interior of the constraints, which prevents the identifi-cation of optimal exploration strategies. At the end of the day, we are left to face an MDP with masked actions, a masked MDP, if you will, with the problem of how to efficiently explore it to identify the mask. Motivated by the computational cost of the mask itself, we focus on 0-noise feedback, analogous to membership-query and realizable-classification set-tings (Angluin, 1988; Littlestone, 1988). We will show, with Theorem 2.3, that this is sufficient to achieve op-timal regret if one must also learn the MDP dynamics and/or rewards. While the mask confesses its values freely, the problem of reaching the information effi-ciently through the MDP dynamics remains. In the remainder of this section, we detail the set-ting explicitly, and formally introduce the problem of mask learning. Thereafter, in Section 2, we establish a minimax-optimal algorithm based on Optimism in the Face of Uncertainty (OFU), and argue that this is not a good benchmark for this problem. In Section 3, we thus study finer, instance-dependent bounds, and in-troduce an algorithm which improves the exploration of OFU on non-degenerate instances, and connect it to the information-directed sampling (IDS) algorithm of Russo and Van Roy (2014), also identifying an aux-iliary MDP formulation, `a la Hao and Lattimore (2022). Finally, in Section 4, we discuss an example application to transfer learning of masks in MDPs, mo-tivated by an industrial application.

# 2 MINIMAX MASK LEARNING 

On the masked MDP, we can consider three main learning problems: the mask, the dynamics, and the combination of both. In this section, we draw a gen-eral picture of the mask learning problem with broad strokes, focusing on minimax-optimality. 

2.1 Learning problems on masked MDPs 

Clearly, if the mask is known but the dynamics must be learnt, the problem is statistically indistinguishable from a classical RL problem (Jin et al., 2020). By com-parison, we will focus on the other two problems where the mask is unknown, due to the absence of its pre-computation, and an agent observes only sequences of state-action-reward-mask quadruples. Meanwhile, the learning problem of the dynamics is naturally for-mulated as an episodic online RL problem (Jin et al., 2020), wherein visiting a state-action pair ( s, a ) reveals a noisy signal of the reward and a sampled transition from P (s, a, ·). Notice that, due to the presence of a cemetery state, one must consider an episodic setting, rather than a non-episodic one. Let u⋆ denote the unknown true parameter of the mask’s linear form. # 4 APPLICATION TO TRANSFER LEARNING 

In RL, beyond single tasks, the literature has increas-ingly aimed to improve computational efficiency by learning multiple instances at once or sequentially. The former has generally been studied in zero-shot 

RL Touati et al. (2022), while the latter falls under 

transfer learning . Given a learnt source and unlearnt target task, transfer aims to leverage shared struc-tures to improve learning, see Pan and Yang (2009) for a broad survey. In RL, the common method is to pre-train representations of environments on large datasets, and then fine-tune them on small-data tar-get tasks (Yosinski et al., 2014). There have been a number of proposed representations: learnt policies (Fern´ andez and Veloso, 2006), value functions (Tay-lor and Stone, 2007), latent state features (Parisotto et al., 2015), successor features (Barreto et al., 2017, 2018), samples (Lazaric et al., 2008), or models of the environment (Sun et al., 2021). In all cases, the diffi-culty lies not only in transferring the correct informa-tion, but also in how to spread learning cost between source and target. Applied to masked MDPs, we seek to transfer infor-mation about two masks for the same linear MDP. The medium of transfer we propose is to choose the set C0 of VSO and λ-planning from information ob-tained about a prior mask in three experimental frame-works. Throughout, the base MDP always has 10 states, 5 actions (one of which aborts the MDP in-dependently of the mask), ϱ = δs1 , and horizon 10. The transfer procedure is repeated for 1000 random MDPs and masks, all figures report averaging on these samples. At each random trial, independently for each pair ( s, a ), we draw three coordinates of ϕ(s, a )uniformly, and draw values for them according to a 

Dirichlet (1 , 1, 1) distribution, setting the rest to 0. The function θ : S → Rd is 0 everywhere except at two uniformly drawn states, with respective weights 

w and 1 − w, for w ∼ Unif [0 , 1]. Meanwhile, the re-ward parameter θr is drawn uniformly in [0 , 1] d and the source mask is drawn uniformly at random on the unit disk in the hyperplane orthogonal to the vector of ones. 5 We take this disk as C0 in what follows. Our three experiments differ in the sampling process of the target mask with respect to the source u(1) which is sampled uniformly in C0. All transfer version spaces will be intersected with C0 to bring the parameter back into the hyperplane. Let ⊕ denote the Minkowski sum: 

A ⊕ B = {a + b : a ∈ A, b ∈ B}.

# 5 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we study masked MDPs with unknown action validity and exact linear score feedback. Our separation result gives conditions under which mask-learning costs enter additively when rewards and tran-sitions are also unknown. Version-space optimism achieves minimax-optimal regret, however, minimax optimality can conceal substantial differences in the cost of acquiring information. To exploit these differences, we introduced λ-planning, which plans exploration through an auxiliary plan-ning problem. Its clearing price yields an instance dependent regret bound and connects the method to information-directed sampling for deterministic feed-back. Transfer experiments illustrate how version spaces preserve useful information across changing masks and how planned exploration can produce gains that accumulate across tasks. AI use statement

# D ACTION OPTIMISM - EXTENDED VALUE ITERATION 

Version-space optimism requires finding a single consistent mask under which the optimal value is maximal. Although planning for a fixed mask is straightforward, searching over compatible masks can be costly. This motivates a relaxation that applies optimism locally, in the spirit of extended value iteration: retain an action whenever some parameter in the current version space makes it valid. Once these action sets are constructed, the policy is obtained by a single backward value-iteration pass. This relaxation avoids a separate planning problem for each compatible mask.

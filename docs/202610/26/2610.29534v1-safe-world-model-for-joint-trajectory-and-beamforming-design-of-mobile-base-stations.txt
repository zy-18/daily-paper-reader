# Safe World Model for Joint Trajectory and Beamforming Design of Mobile Base Stations 

Abdolrasoul Sakhaei Gharagezlou, Zeinab Askari Donbeh, Mehdi Monemi, Matti Latva-aho, Mehdi Rasti 

Abstract —Mobile base stations (MBSs), including aerial and ground-based platforms, provide flexible wireless coverage through adaptive positioning. However, jointly optimizing their trajectories and digital beamforming is challenging because movement changes channel conditions and interference, while beamforming determines the resulting signal and interference powers. These coupled decisions also account for how current movements affect future positions and communication perfor-mance under mobility and safety constraints. We formulate this problem as a constrained Markov decision process that maximizes the time-averaged sum signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR), subject to mobility, restricted-zone avoidance, and transmit-power constraints. To support long-term planning and reduce the extensive environment interactions required by model-free reinforcement learning, we develop a SafeDreamer-based world model framework that learns latent system dy-namics from experience and optimizes a joint trajectory and beamforming policy through imagined trajectories. Feasibility-preserving action mappings and safety cost signals incorporate operational constraints into policy learning. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework improves the time-averaged sum SINR by an average of 7% and 9% compared with proximal policy and deep Q-network baselines, respectively, while achieving higher sample efficiency and more reliable constraint satisfaction. 

Index Terms —Mobile base station (MBS), reinforcement learn-ing (RL), world model, SafeDreamer. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# MOBILE base stations (MBSs), including unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and ground-based mobile plat-forms, have emerged as a promising solution for enhancing wireless coverage and communication performance through flexible deployment and adaptive mobility, particularly in areas with limited network infrastructure or temporary communica-tion needs [1], [2]. However, jointly optimizing MBS trajec-tories and beamforming is challenging due to time-varying channels, mobility constraints, and temporal dependencies. Extensive research has investigated MBS-assisted commu-nications to improve security, coverage, and throughput [1]– [6]. For example, the authors in [1] studied age-of-information minimization in UAV-assisted covert communications in the presence of an aerial eavesdropper. The authors in [2] de-veloped a secure UAV-enabled integrated sensing and com-munication (ISAC) framework to maximize the secrecy rate, whereas [3] investigated achievable-rate maximization in a reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-assisted UAV-ISAC system. Despite these advances, conventional optimization methods often require accurate wireless environment models and incur high computational complexity. Such models can be difficult to obtain under time-varying channel conditions and

MBS mobility. Furthermore, their high computational com-plexity limits real-time implementation. Although heuristic methods can alleviate these challenges, their scalability and robustness may remain limited in large-scale and dynamic wireless environments [7]. To overcome these limitations, recent studies have adopted reinforcement learning (RL) approaches, including model-based and model-free RL (MBRL/MFRL), to optimize system performance in dynamic wireless networks without requiring accurate mathematical models of the underlying network dy-namics. For example, the authors in [4] proposed a hierarchical deep RL framework to enhance the performance of over-the-air federated learning systems in UAV-assisted wireless networks. The authors in [5] employed MBRL and MFRL for energy efficiency (EE) maximization in mmWave UAV networks, while the authors in [6] used proximal policy optimization (PPO) and deep deterministic policy gradient for sum-rate and EE maximization in multi-UAV networks. Nevertheless, existing RL-based approaches often suffer from limited sam-ple efficiency , requiring extensive environment interactions to explore high-dimensional action spaces, while their limited long-term planning capabilities may hinder the optimization of temporally coupled trajectory and beamforming decisions. World-model-based RL (WMBRL) offers a promising so-lution to these challenges by learning a compact represen-tation of environmental dynamics to predict future states and optimize policies through imagined trajectories [7]. This enables sample-efficient policy optimization while capturing the long-term effects of trajectory and beamforming decisions. Such long-term planning is crucial for maximizing the time-averaged sum signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR), as MBS mobility influences both instantaneous communica-tion performance and future channel conditions while affecting trajectory feasibility. Nevertheless, the integration of long-term planning and safety-aware learning for trajectory and beamforming optimization in dynamic MBS-assisted wireless networks remains largely unexplored. Motivated by the above research gap, the main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. First, we formu-late the joint trajectory and digital beamforming optimiza-tion problem for MBSs as a constrained Markov decision process to maximize the time-averaged sum SINR under time-varying channel conditions, MBS mobility, restricted-zone (RZ), and transmit-power constraints, while accounting for the temporal dependencies of MBS trajectories. Second, we develop a SafeDreamer-based WMBRL framework that learns network dynamics from collected experience and uses predicted sequences of network states to jointly optimize MBS trajectories and digital beamforming, enabling long-term planning while reducing the environment interactions required arXiv:2610.29534v1 [eess.SY] 21 Oct 2026 2

for policy learning. Third, we incorporate the constraints into policy learning through feasibility-preserving action mappings and safety-cost signals. Finally, simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves higher time-averaged sum SINR than the considered benchmarks, while exhibiting improved sample efficiency and more reliable constraint sat-isfaction compared with PPO and deep Q-network (DQN). B. Constraint Handling 

The constraints in problem (4) exhibit different char-acteristics and therefore require distinct constraint-handling approaches. Specifically, the MBS mobility and maximum transmit-power constraints are enforced through action-space construction. The initial-position constraint is satisfied by setting the MBS at its predefined starting location, while the final-position constraint is enforced through feasibility-preserving trajectory projection to ensure that the MBS reaches its destination within the prescribed mission duration. Further-more, the operating-region and RZ constraints are incorporated into safety-cost learning.

C. Learning Method 

The proposed WMBRL framework consists of three main components: (i) a world model that learns a compact la-tent representation from high-dimensional observations and predicts future states, rewards, and safety costs; (ii) reward and cost critics that estimate the expected long-term com-munication performance and associated safety costs under a given policy; (iii) a safe actor that selects actions to maximize the expected long-term reward while satisfying the prescribed safety constraints. These components are detailed in the following subsections. 4

1) World Model Learning: The world model comprises an observation encoder, a recurrent state-space model (RSSM), and prediction heads for the observations, rewards, safety costs, and continuation signals. The observation encoder maps the high-dimensional observation o[t] into a compact latent representation, while the RSSM models its temporal evolution based on the agent’s action a[t]. Specifically, at each time slot t, the deterministic state h[t] is updated based on the previous deterministic state h[t − 1] , stochastic state z[t − 1] ,and action a[t − 1] , while the stochastic state z[t] is inferred from the current deterministic state h[t] and observation o[t].The RSSM models the environment dynamics through its sequence model and transition prior, enabling the generation of imagined trajectories without direct environment interactions. The resulting latent states are used to reconstruct observations and predict rewards, safety costs, and continuation signals. The SL scheme follows a direct trajectory with uniformly spaced positions. In addition, an upper-bound (UB) benchmark is established based on the minimum geometrically achievable distances between the MBS and the users. Fig. 1a shows that the proposed WMBRL framework achieves higher time-averaged sum SINR and faster con-vergence than PPO and DQN by optimizing policies over imagined trajectories, reducing the need for direct environment interactions. In contrast, DQN requires more interactions to converge, while PPO converges more slowly to a lower SINR. In this paper, we investigated the joint optimization of MBS trajectories and digital beamforming to maximize the time-averaged sum SINR under mobility, RZ, and transmit-power constraints. We developed a SafeDreamer-based WMBRL framework that learns system dynamics and optimizes the control policy over imagined trajectories, while incorporating constraints through action mappings and safety-cost signals.
